Section B · Brand Identity

Voice.

How the brand sounds. Not a tone chart. A living calibration system built from real writing.

SectionB1 StatusConfirmed SourceBrand Voice + Exemplars
01
Calibration

Five traits.

Witty

French sarcasm meets Californian lightness. The humor is always present: goofy when the moment calls for it, dry when it doesn't.

  • Sounds like: "Building a brand is emotionally violent. Most people would rather just pick a color palette."
  • Not like: "Humor is a powerful branding tool that can help you build authentic connections with your audience!"

Unhurried

Observes before speaking. Lets the idea breathe. Short sentences to anchor, long ones to reflect. No rushing to the point.

  • Sounds like: "Sounds simple, but it's not easy."
  • Not like: "Here are 7 quick tips to nail your brand voice today."

Real

Sounds like a person talking. Wonders out loud. Admits not knowing. Genuine confusion beats manufactured certainty.

  • Sounds like: "Branding is really about small details. The ones nobody notices until they're wrong."
  • Not like: "Our proven methodology has helped hundreds of founders unlock their brand potential."

Confident

Speaks from experience, not from a need to prove anything. Quiet authority. Never prescriptive first.

  • Sounds like: "You are already doing branding. It's up to you if you want to control that narrative."
  • Not like: "Trust me, I've been doing this for 10+ years and I can tell you exactly what you need."

Nuanced

Nothing is binary. Holds multiple possibilities. Opens the perspective instead of closing it.

  • Sounds like: "Branding sometimes is not just about what's in front of you. You have to use your gut feelings."
  • Not like: "There are only two types of founders: those who invest in branding and those who fail."
02
Word choice

Vocabulary swaps.

Specific word choices that keep the voice consistent across every surface.

  • "brand" not "company." Reads broader. Fits solopreneur to SME. Use "company" only when org-chart scale is the actual topic.
  • "the Brand Therapy process" not "Brand Therapy helps..." Methodology, not company. Never personify it.
  • "noticed" not "believe." Observation voice. "I noticed" is grounded. "I believe" is prescriptive.
  • "... (ellipsis)" not em dash. Ellipses create breathing room. Em dashes are banned.
  • "perspective shift" not "lesson." We shift how people see things. We don't lecture.
03
Territory

Language territory.

Preferred words

These live naturally in the brand's vocabulary. Use freely.

  • clarity, paradox, process, attention, humor, design
  • perspective, instinct, psychology, elegance
  • perception, culture, contradiction, texture, connection, purpose

Use with care

Only when earned by context. These words become hollow fast.

  • growth, success, authenticity, mindset
  • passion, leadership, inspiration, impact
04
Rules

Voice principles.

  • Direct observation over encouragement
  • Dry wit over cheerleading
  • Spoken register, not editorial
  • Questions over affirmations. Turn statements into invitations the reader answers
  • Short sentences when possible. Long when the idea demands breath
  • Silence is an option
  • Curious over prescriptive. Ask the question, observe the pattern
  • Prefer "brand" over "company" when ambiguity helps
  • Don't overclaim what the Brand Therapy process does
  • Bold the load-bearing phrases. Two to four per section in long-form
05
Guardrails

Always / Never.

Always

  • Use ellipsis, colon, comma, or new sentence where an em dash would go
  • Ellipses (...) freely for rhythm, irony, breathing room. Three dots, no space before, one space after
  • Refer to "the Brand Therapy process" or "Brand Therapy work"
  • State corrections directly when wrong
  • Match the founder's energy when they get playful
  • Peer voice default: "we/us" not "founders" or "every founder I know"
  • End with a perspective shift, not a lesson
  • Vary sentence rhythm: short to anchor, long to reflect

Never

  • Em dashes or en dashes
  • Emojis in brand content
  • Brand Therapy as a company
  • "Personal brand" as lead framing
  • Bullet-point advice lists as main structure
  • Preachy, motivational, or hollow affirmations
  • Fabricated claims about named people
  • Fabricated empirical generalizations ("most founders...", invented stats)
  • Overly constructed paragraphs, essay-style topic sentences
  • Formal connectives: "however," "as a result," "in conclusion," "it's worth noting"
06
Structure

Structural patterns.

Affirmations to questions

Core pattern. When tempted to state something as fact, turn it into a question the reader answers themselves. "Nobody opens a gift they're not attracted to" becomes "Would you open a gift you're not attracted to... or even suspicious of?" Questions invite. Affirmations close.

Rhythm

Short to anchor. Long to reflect. Alternate. Never three long sentences in a row.

Openers

Three default modes: observation, curiosity, or admission. Never encouragement.

Punctuation

  • Ellipsis: Freely, for rhythm, irony, breathing room. Three dots, no space before, one space after
  • Colon: For lists and setups. Replaces em dashes
  • Period: Preferred over semicolons. Two short sentences beat one long compound

Closers

End with a perspective shift, not a lesson. Leave the reader thinking, not nodding.

Checks in

"Right?" as invitation, not lecture. Short incomplete sentences followed by grounded explanations.

Duality

Switch lenses naturally: client vs. agency, creator vs. consumer, logic vs. emotion. Find clarity in paradox. Every idea has its shadow.

07
Channels

Content type adaptations.

LinkedIn

Hook under 100 chars. Follow-up line that reframes. Conversational body (3-6 short paragraphs). Perspective shift ending. Never end with a CTA. 150-300 words. Peak personality. Dad jokes earn their place here.

Opening moves: sharp industry observation, personal admission, question that reframes a common assumption.

Newsletter

One idea per edition. Opens like writing to a smart friend. Can go longer, more reflective. Sections with bolded load-bearing phrases (2-4 per section). 500-1200 words. Most conversational channel.

Opening moves: something that happened this week, a question a client asked, an observation that's been sitting with you.

Website

Lead with what the reader gets, not who Florian is. Witty with care. Smart humor that highlights value. Every section earns its place. Headlines under 8 words. Subheads conversational.

Opening moves: reframe an expectation, ask the reader's real question before they do.

Proposal

Professional confidence dialed up. Wit is subtle, not central. Clarity and composure dominate. Shorter paragraphs than newsletter. Every sentence does work.

Opening moves: name the situation the client described, restate their words back to them.

08
QA

Quality gate.

Every piece of brand content passes through these checks before shipping.

  • Could Florian say this out loud without cringing?
  • At least one moment of wit or unexpected phrasing?
  • Ends with perspective, not a lesson?
  • Would someone know this wasn't written by ChatGPT?
  • Any em dashes? Replace every one
  • Does any sentence put Florian in first chair? Rewrite per Host/Messenger
  • Any fabricated claims about named people? Remove or soften to influence-language
09
Avoid

Anti-patterns.

If the content sounds like any of these, it's off-voice. Rewrite.

  • Self-help book chapter
  • Corporate newsletter
  • Therapist's Instagram caption
  • Hustle-culture motivational post
  • AI-generated thought leadership piece
  • First-chair founder bio ("Meet Florian," "In his own words")
  • Fabricated attributions or empirical claims
10
Mechanics

Style rules.

  • Oxford comma: No, unless needed for clarity
  • Ellipsis: Three dots, no space before, one space after. Never four dots
  • Numbers: Spell out one through nine, numerals for 10+. Statistics always use numerals
  • Capitalization: Sentence case for headlines and subheads. Title Case only for proper nouns and Brand Therapy
  • Quotation marks: Double for dialogue/direct quotes. Single for quotes-within-quotes or ironic emphasis
