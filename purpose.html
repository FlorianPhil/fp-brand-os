Section A · P3 · Brand Strategy

Purpose.

Make every brand unique. Florian's work exists to make every brand unique by revealing what is already specific and turning it into decisions, not by making brands louder, prettier, or generically marketable.

SpecificitySelectionLess interchangeableUseful distinction
The purpose is not to make brands louder. It is to make them harder to confuse.
01
Strategic decision

The operating choice.

This is the decision this P owns. Everything downstream should preserve it.

Core decision

Purpose

Florian's work exists to make every brand unique by revealing what is already specific and turning it into decisions, not by making brands louder, prettier, or generically marketable.

Interpretation

Plain meaning

Florian does not invent uniqueness. He finds what is already specific, makes it legible, and turns it into decisions the brand can keep using.

Cost of choice

Tradeoff

This favors sharper fit over broad appeal. It may reduce possible audiences, but increases the value of the right audience.

02
Intelligence layer

What AI must know.

These fields are compact enough for AI context and precise enough for design and marketing decisions.

Purpose Line

Make every brand unique.

Strategic Belief

Specificity already exists in the person, work, culture, standards, or point of view before it becomes copy or design.

Decision Filters

  • Name what is specifically true and what that excludes.
  • Use content to reveal useful distinctions, hidden tensions, wrong comparisons, and the cost of looking generic.
  • Choose collaborators, clients, and channels that sharpen the point of view.
  • Use design to make specificity visible, not merely decorative.

Story Prompts

  • Where generic positioning hides real value.
  • What the brand refuses to become.
  • Why stronger fit often starts with fewer audiences, fewer claims, and fewer borrowed references.

Creative Controls

  • Show focused selection, edited sets, contrast, or precise choices.
  • Signal subtraction and specificity.
  • Avoid crowds, funnels, megaphones, rocket ships, growth charts, and vague uniqueness symbols.
03
Rules

What belongs and what does not.

A real strategy excludes things. These are the hard filters for copy, design, content, and sales.

Rules in

Belongs

  • Specificity already exists in the person, work, culture, standards, or point of view.
  • Strategy should reveal and sharpen, not invent a costume.
  • Growth comes from better matches, clearer noes, and stronger fit.
  • A brand should become easier to choose because it is less interchangeable.
Rules out

Does not belong

  • More marketing activity as the default answer.
  • Trend imitation.
  • Generic best-practice positioning.
  • A brand that tries to appeal to everyone.
  • Uniqueness as decoration instead of decision-making.
04
Marketing use

Triggers, proof, and output controls.

This is the AI-readable layer for website copy, landing pages, sales, content, visual direction, and image prompts.

Triggers

When it matters

  • Where generic positioning hides real value.
  • What the brand refuses to become.
  • Why stronger fit often starts with fewer audiences, fewer claims, and fewer borrowed references.
Proof required

Show evidence

  • Examples of saying no to wrong-fit opportunities.
  • Strategy decisions that make a brand more specific and less comparable.
  • Evidence that specificity improves clarity, confidence, referrals, or conversion quality.
AI rule

AI behavior

Do not generate generic differentiation language. Name what is uniquely true and what it excludes.

Output control

Copy

Use uniqueness as a strategic filter, not a motivational slogan.

Output control

Visual

Show subtraction, focus, and specificity. Avoid generic growth imagery.

Output control

Image

A focused selection, edited set, or clear mark of distinction. No crowd, funnel, or generic growth-chart imagery.
